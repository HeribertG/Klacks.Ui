Resource Monitor — Manual

What does the chart show?

The Resource Monitor visualizes at a glance how workforce utilization stands for the selected group (or all groups) in the chosen year. One thin bar per day — across 365 days a full annual trajectory.

The visualization follows an established industry pattern: the "Capacity vs. Workload Chart" from HR/workforce planning. This chart type is used in comparable form by HR systems like SAP SuccessFactors, Workday and Personio. In project management literature (PMI/PMBOK) the variant is known as "Resource Histogram". Anyone familiar with one of these tools will immediately recognize the concept: stacked bars for consumption + lost capacity, combined with reference lines for target and maximum capacity.

The elements in detail

Green bars — Services (Dienste)

Number of scheduled services on this day. Each shift counts as 1 employee slot. Only regular shifts are counted: time-fixed, non-sporadic shifts within their validity range. Container shifts count as 1 (sub-tasks inside the container are not double-counted).

Gray bars — Absences (Absenzen)

Number of absent employees, stacked above the green bars. A full vacation or sickness entry counts as 1 employee, a half-day entry as 0.5. Vacation and sickness also count on weekends — values are taken as entered in the absence planner.

Pink dotted line — Desired Readiness

How many employees would on average be daily available if the desired number of work days per week is respected (standard: 5 work days, 2 rest days). This is the planning target.

For a 24/7 contract (all 7 weekdays flagged as possible work days) the value is smoothed across the entire week: per employee 5 / 7 ≈ 0.71 contribution per calendar day. A Mo–Fr contract shows 1.0 on weekdays and 0 on weekends.

Red dashed line — Max. Readiness

The physical/legal maximum: how many employees would be available if each works the maximum number of consecutive days allowed (standard: 6 days, at least 1 rest day). Always at or above the pink line.

Blue line — Total Employees

Total headcount with active contract on this day. Independent of weekday flags and work-day caps. Only changes at contract starts or ends.

How to read it

Workforce optimum

When the top edge of the green bars (services) approaches the pink dotted line (desired readiness), the workforce is optimally deployed — exactly as many services as the plan calls for.

Reserve and warning signal

The gap between the total headcount (blue) and the top of the stacked bars (services + absences) shows the reserve.

Rule of thumb: the top of the gray absence bars should not exceed one third of the gap between services and total headcount. Beyond that, operational reserve is endangered — sickness/vacation peaks make filling open shifts difficult.

Group and year filter

The arrow buttons at top right switch the year. The group selector restricts to one location/team — all values then refer only to shifts and employees of that group. "All Groups" shows the overall view.

Source logic in the backend

Values are estimates and approximations, not rigid math — they give an impression of availability:

  • Services: from the Shift table (planning), not Work (realization). This makes future services visible too.
  • Absences: from the BreakPlaceholder table (planned absence ranges) weighted by Absence.DefaultValue.
  • Availability (pink/red): from ClientContract + Contract.SchedulingRule. The caps (Max. Work Days / Max. Consecutive Days) are first taken from the contract's scheduling rule, otherwise from the global settings.
  • Total headcount (blue): number of employees with an active contract on this day.
